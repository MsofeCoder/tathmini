Tathmini — Stakeholder Demo Guide
How to present the Phase 1 prototype · 15 minutes · one presenter, one screen
THE ONE THING TO LAND
A supervisor can assess a full trainee in the field with no internet, and the College gets the result the moment there is signal. Everything else in the demo is evidence for that sentence.

Before the room fills

1 Open the prototype and click Reset demo in the top bar. This restores the 8 trainees, clears drafts and pending reports, and returns you to the welcome screen. Do this before every run — a half-finished state is the most common way a demo goes wrong.
2 Confirm the top bar reads Online and Supervisor · mobile is the active tab.
3 Have this guide on a second screen or on paper. Do not read the demo off the main screen — the audience should watch the app, not your cursor hunting.
4 Decide which trainee you will assess and stick to it. Use Asha R. Mwinyi (first card, TP track, not yet assessed). Scoring is 60 taps if you do it exhaustively — see step 5 of Act 2 for the shortcut.
SAY THIS UP FRONT, ONCE
“This is a working prototype, not the finished system. The scoring, grading and offline behaviour you are about to see are real and final. The criterion wording is drafted from Annex IV and needs the College's verbatim text before we pilot.” Saying it first stops it becoming a gotcha later.

Act 1 — Identity and access · 90 seconds

1 Start on the welcome screen. Let them see the College crest and the install prompt.
“Tathmini installs to the supervisor's home screen like any app — but there is nothing to download from a store, and it updates itself.”
2 Tap Add to Home Screen. On the sign-in screen, point at the credentials box.
“There is no self-registration. The Administrator issues every account at induction — username is firstname.lastname, first password is lastname123, changed on first sign-in.”
This is the answer to “who can get in?” — give it before it is asked.
3 Tap Sign in.

Act 2 — The supervisor's real day · 6 minutes, the heart of the demo

1 Establish the route. Point at the progress tracker: “2 of 8 trainees assessed · 25%”, then the three counters below it.
“A supervisor sees only their own route. That is not a screen setting — the database refuses to return another route's trainees.”
2 Show the search. Type weld — Emmanuel surfaces with the match highlighted. Clear it.
“Search covers name, trade, centre and status, and it runs on the device — so it works with no signal.”
3 Go offline now, not later. Click Offline in the top bar. The banner appears; nothing else changes.
“I am now going to do the entire assessment with no internet. Watch for anything that stops working.”
This framing is what makes the offline claim land — you are inviting them to catch you out.
4 Open Asha R. Mwinyi. Note occupation and track are pre-loaded and read-only, and the assessment is recorded under the route owner.
“The supervisor never chooses which form to use. The trainee's track decides it, so the wrong instrument cannot be filled in.”
Tap Begin Theory assessment.
5 Demonstrate the gate — do this deliberately. Score only the first criterion, then tap Next. The red alert names how many are still unmarked.
“You cannot skip a criterion. An unmarked criterion is not a zero — it is an incomplete assessment, and the system will not let it through.”
6 Now score Section 1 fully. Give the last criterion a low mark on purpose — 0 on “Teaching and learning aids prepared in advance and relevant” — so you have an auto-comment to show later. Note the amber flag that appears under it.
Point at the two progress bars: the thin one is this form, the amber one is both forms together. The “Section subtotal” line above is where this section stands.
7 Show draft recovery. Tap Trainees in the bottom nav, mid-assessment. Asha's card now carries a DRAFT badge. Reopen her — Tathmini offers Resume draft or Start over. Resume.
“A supervisor's phone dies, or they close the app to take a call. Nothing is lost — every tap was already written to the device.”
8 Work through the remaining sections. Use the Sections ⌄ jump list to show where you are rather than narrating every tap — the audience does not need to watch 60 taps.
Pace tip: tap quickly and keep talking over it. Silence during scoring is where a demo dies. If time is tight, say “I will score the rest at speed” and do so.
9 The comments step — spend real time here. Show the suggestion in plain English, then tap Add suggestion to my comment. It lands in the supervisor's own box as an ordinary sentence. Edit a word in front of them.
“The system drafts the advice — in language a trainee can act on, not scores and jargon. But it is the supervisor's comment. They own it, they can rewrite it, and the trainee reads one voice.”
This is the feature assessors will judge you on. Do not rush it.

Act 3 — Review, preview, submit · 3 minutes

1 On the review screen, walk the verdict band, then Theory and Practical: total, grade, GPA, Class of Award.
“Grade, GPA and Class of Award are computed from the College's own key — A above 80, B above 65, C above 50, and Competent at 50 per cent combined. Nobody adds up a column by hand, and nobody can disagree about the arithmetic.”
2 Tap Preview the PDF report. Let them read it for a few seconds in silence.
“This is the exact document that reaches the trainee and the College — on College letterhead, against the Annex IV form. The supervisor sees it before committing, not after.”
Close it.
3 Tap Submit Result. The confirmation sheet lists the three consequences.
“Submitting locks the record, generates the PDF and emails three people. That is irreversible, so we say so plainly rather than hiding it behind a button.”
Confirm.
4 The payoff. The screen reads “Saved — waiting for internet.” Remind them you have been offline since Act 2, step 3.
“Sixty criteria, two forms, a PDF and a locked record — all with no signal. The report is on the device and it will send itself.”
Point at the celebration card: “And the supervisor gets told what their work achieved, and how much of the route is left. This is a job people do alone, in the field, all day.”
5 Tap View attachment to show the finished report, then open Pending in the bottom nav — one report waiting, plainly stated.
6 Switch to Online. The queue drains in front of them and the route tracker ticks up.
“No button was pressed. The supervisor drives back into coverage and the College has the result.”

Act 4 — Reassignment · 90 seconds

1 Open Moves in the bottom nav — badged with one incoming request from Route 03.
2 Tap Accept. Go back to Trainees — Rehema is on the route and the counts have changed.
“Trainees move between routes in real life. Two supervisors settle it between themselves — the Coordinator is not a bottleneck, but they can see every move.”
3 If asked how one starts: open any trainee → Request cross-route reassignment → pick a route, add a reason, send. Note that only the receiving supervisor gets Accept and Reject.

Act 5 — The Coordinator's view · 3 minutes

1 Click Coordinator · desktop. Let the six route cards speak for themselves.
“One screen, every route, updating live. Today this is a phone call to each supervisor.”
2 Open Route 04 — Morogoro Urban. Sort by COMBINED, filter to Not yet assessed, then clear it.
“Sort, filter, search — and Export CSV for the College's own records.”
Click Export CSV if a real file landing is useful to your audience; skip it if the download dialog will interrupt your flow.
3 Open Asha R. Mwinyi — the trainee you just assessed. Show the section breakdown, then Open the PDF result report.
“The result the supervisor submitted from the field, in the College's hands, with the report attached.”
4 Close on governance. Click Administrator override. Show that a reason is required before Confirm unlock will activate.
“A submitted result is read-only, including to the supervisor who wrote it. Correcting one is deliberate, reasoned, and recorded against the Administrator's name. That is the audit trail the College needs.”
This is a strong final note for an accreditation-minded audience. End here.

Answers to the questions you will be asked

“What if the phone is lost or stolen?” Anything already sent is on the College's servers. Anything pending is on that device only — which is why the app tells the supervisor plainly how many reports are waiting, and warns them before sign-out. Device-level encryption and remote sign-out are Phase 2.
“Can a supervisor change a mark after submitting?” No. The database rejects the write, not just the screen. Only an Administrator can reopen it, with a reason, on the record.
“Could a supervisor see another route's trainees?” No. Route scoping is enforced by row-level security on the server. Even a modified app would get nothing back.
“Is the criterion wording final?” No — and say so cleanly. Section names, point totals and criterion counts follow Annex IV exactly. The individual sentences are drafts; the College's verbatim text drops in before pilot.
“What about the IPT track?” The authentic IPT instrument has not been supplied. Open Emmanuel or Daniel and show the amber warning the app itself displays. Honesty here builds more confidence than a silent placeholder.
“Is it in Swahili?” Not yet. It is scoped and straightforward; the decision needed from the College is whether the criteria themselves are translated or only the interface.
“When can we pilot?” Give the two things you need, not a date: the verbatim Annex IV wording and the IPT instrument. Then the build is deployment, not design.
“Does it work on any phone?” Any reasonably current Android or iPhone with a browser. No app store, no installs to manage, no per-device licence.

The harder questions — by who asks them

Answer in one or two sentences, then stop. A short answer sounds like a decided system; a long one sounds like a defence.
PRINCIPAL / ACADEMIC BOARD — is this defensible?
“Who owns the assessment decision — the supervisor or the software?” The supervisor, without exception. The system awards no marks. It records the marks, applies the VETA grading key, and refuses to accept an incomplete form. The competency verdict is arithmetic on the supervisor's own scoring.
“Does this replace the Annex IV paper form?” It reproduces it. Same sections, same maxima, same 100 points, same signature lines on the printed report. For pilot, run both in parallel if the Board prefers — the app produces the paper.
“If VETA audits us, what do we hand over?” The signed report per trainee, plus a record of who scored it, when, from where, and every administrator override with its stated reason. That is more than a paper file gives you.
“What if VETA changes the grading key or the criteria?” Criteria and bands are configuration, not code. Changing them is an afternoon, and past results keep the key that was in force when they were signed.
SUPERVISORS — will this make my day harder?
“I have no data bundle in the villages.” You do not need one to assess. Everything is marked on the phone. The report waits in Pending and sends itself the moment you find network — at the college, at your home, anywhere.
“My battery dies mid-assessment. Do I start again?” No. Every tap is saved as you go. Reopen the trainee and you continue from the same criterion.
“The system writes my comment for me — is that not dishonest?” It writes a draft, into your box, which you edit or delete. It exists so a low score is never sent without advice attached. The words that go to the trainee are the words you approve.
“I am not good with phones.” There is one screen at a time and one thing to tap on it. Nothing can be broken by tapping the wrong button — the app will not let you submit something wrong.
“I cannot reach my trainee — sickness, distance, transport.” Reassign them to a colleague from inside the app. The Coordinator sees the request, the receiving supervisor accepts, and the trainee is not left unassessed.
COORDINATOR / QA — can I trust the numbers?
“How do I know a supervisor actually visited?” Each submission carries its device, time and account. GPS stamping at submit is a Phase 2 option the College can switch on — say it is available, not that it is built.
“Two supervisors mark very differently. Will I see that?” You already see per-route competent rates and every individual score. Per-supervisor comparison is a report we can add once there is real data worth comparing.
“Can I get the results into Excel?” Yes — export to CSV from the dashboard, and print or email any single report as PDF.
ICT / BURSAR — what does it cost us to run?
“Where does the data live?” A managed database owned by the College, with automatic backups. Nothing of value lives only on a phone once it has been sent.
“Who administers accounts?” The College. Accounts are created by the Administrator with a set password — there is no public sign-up, so no stranger can create a supervisor account.
“Recurring cost?” Hosting, database and email delivery at this volume — modest and predictable. No app-store fees, no per-device licence, no per-supervisor seat.
“What if the developer leaves?” The code and database are the College's, on standard tools any competent developer can maintain. Handover documentation is part of the delivery.
THREE SENTENCES TO FALL BACK ON
— “The supervisor assesses. The system only makes sure nothing is missed, nothing is lost, and nothing is changed afterwards.”
— “It works with no network, because that is where our trainees are.”
— “Everything you see follows Annex IV and the VETA competent / not competent decision. Where it does not yet, I will tell you which part.”
DO NOT DO THESE
— Do not open a locked trainee (Baraka, Neema) expecting to assess them. The button is disabled by design and you will look stuck.
— Do not skip Reset demo between runs.
— Do not narrate every tap while scoring. Talk about what the system prevents while your hands work.
— Do not promise a feature you have not shown. Write the request down instead — a presenter who says “that is not built, I am noting it” is trusted more than one who says yes to everything.
